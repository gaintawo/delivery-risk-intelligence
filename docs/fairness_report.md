# Fairness Metrics Report

DRI late-delivery classifier, MLflow model version 3 · Test period, purchases June–August 2018 (18,603 delivered orders) · BAN6800 Module 4

Generated by `src/models/analyze.py` with Fairlearn 0.15.0. The source tables are in `docs/evidence/model/fairness_*.csv`.

## 1. What fairness means for this model (Module 2 framework)

Being flagged is **helpful**: a flagged order gets early action. The harm is a late order that is *not* flagged. So the primary metric is **equal opportunity**: recall on late orders should not differ by more than 10 points between regions (goal G5, ethical risk ER1). Demographic parity, equalized odds and the disparate impact ratio are reported as the brief requires. Demographic parity is not a release gate, because regions genuinely differ in how often orders are late.

**Protected attributes.** Olist data contains no age, gender or race. The groups named in the Module 1 Ethical AI Charter are **customer region** (a proxy for income and remoteness, ER1 and ER2) and **seller size** (ER3: small and new sellers must not be over-flagged). Regions with fewer than 30 late orders (the North: 9) are reported but not used to judge the gate, because their recall is too uncertain.

**Decision rule assessed:** the top 25% of each day's orders by risk score are flagged.

**Finding.** The model flags 78% of Northeast orders but only 18% of Southeast orders. As a result it catches 97% of late Northeast orders but only 30% of late Southeast orders, and the Southeast holds 812 of the 1,019 late orders. The bias runs against the Southeast in recall, and against the Northeast in false alarms: 77% of its on-time orders are flagged. This differs from the Module 3 concern, which was under-representation of the North and Northeast. The model has learned that long distances and long recent delivery times mean risk, but Olist already pads promises to those regions, so their late rate (5.4%) is lower than the Southeast's (6.2%).

| Overall effect | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recall on all late orders | 37.6% | 51.1% |

**Result.** The recall gap between judged regions falls from 66.8 to 24.7 points, and overall recall *rises* from 37.6% to 51.1% while fewer orders are flagged. Here fairness and performance improve together, because the single threshold wasted most of the capacity on Northeast orders that arrived on time. The gap still exceeds the 10-point gate, so the model stays in shadow mode.

New sellers are flagged more often (32% vs 22–26%) and have the highest false-positive rate. Because a flag means extra contact from seller support, this is a risk of over-contacting new sellers. Mitigation: flags for new sellers should lead to a supportive check-in, never a penalty (Module 1 charter, principle 5).

## 6. Conclusion and next steps

1. **The G5 gate is not met.** The model must not drive actions beyond shadow mode (Module 2 rollout plan).
2. **Use the ThresholdOptimizer thresholds** for any pilot. They are fairer and they catch more late orders.
3. **Collect more data** for the North, Central-West and South before judging them: their recall intervals are wider than ±12 points.
4. **Address the root cause** in the next version. Model the late rate *relative to the region's promise padding*, and test the XGBoost challenger with the same post-processing.
